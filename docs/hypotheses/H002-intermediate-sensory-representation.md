# H002 — Intermediate sensory representation

- **Status:** unvalidated
- **Owner of decision:** Human

## Hypothesis

Different input modalities may be normalized into a shared intermediate sensory representation before any mapping to sake terminology.

## Rationale and assumptions

A shared intermediate layer may let gesture, sound, rhythm, and onomatopoeia remain replaceable while keeping later language mapping consistent. It may also make experiments easier to compare. This does not establish that one representation can faithfully capture every modality.

## Experiment idea

Create a small local prototype that accepts two modalities and displays the same deliberately provisional sensory representation, then compare whether participants understand and recognize the relationship.

## Success signals

- The same representation can accommodate more than one input path without hiding meaningful differences.
- Participants can explain what the intermediate representation has in common with their inputs.
- Changing an input adapter does not require changing the domain representation.

## Failure signals

- The representation erases distinctions people consider important.
- Participants cannot connect the representation to their input.
- The abstraction adds complexity without improving comparison or communication.

## Evidence and follow-up

No experiment has been recorded yet. Record observations using the [experiment format](../experiments/README.md) before changing this status.
