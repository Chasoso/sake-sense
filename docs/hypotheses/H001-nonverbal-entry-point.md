# H001 — Nonverbal expression as an entry point

- **Status:** unvalidated
- **Owner of decision:** Human

## Hypothesis

People who cannot easily describe sake sensations with words may preserve useful pre-verbal sensations through onomatopoeia, gesture, pointer movement, sound, or rhythm before mapping them toward language.

## Rationale and assumptions

Beginners may notice a sensation before they know the relevant sake vocabulary. A low-pressure expression step might help them externalize that sensation. This assumes the expression is meaningful enough to revisit or share; that is not established.

## Experiment idea

Offer one or more nonverbal input paths for a small, alcohol-free local demo, then invite participants to describe whether the resulting representation helps them talk about the sensation.

## Success signals

- Participants can produce an expression without first learning specialist terms.
- Participants report that the expression preserves or helps recall something about the sensation.
- Participants can move from the expression to some form of shared language.

## Failure signals

- Participants cannot understand what to do without extensive instruction.
- The expression feels arbitrary, embarrassing, or unrelated to the sensation.
- The step adds friction without helping later description or recall.

## Evidence and follow-up

No experiment has been recorded yet. Record observations using the [experiment format](../experiments/README.md) before changing this status.
